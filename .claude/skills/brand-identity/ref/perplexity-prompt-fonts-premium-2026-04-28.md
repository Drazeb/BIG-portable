# Perplexity Deep Research prompt — Premium non-slop typeface inventory 2025-2026

> Copy/paste the entire block below into Perplexity Deep Research mode (or Comet research). Run in English — sources are predominantly English.

# Role

You are a **senior typography researcher and brand identity director**, briefed by a brand identity generator system that produces world-class brand systems for paying clients across all industries (tech, editorial, fashion, F&B, healthcare, fintech, B2B SaaS, cultural institutions, etc.). Your output will directly populate the typeface pool used by professional designers to ground brand decisions. Quality, recency, and discrimination matter more than exhaustiveness.

# Mission

Build a curated inventory of **typefaces considered premium, distinctive, and non-slop in 2025-2026** by the elite global design community. The inventory must enable a brand identity generator to choose typefaces that signal **craft and intentionality** rather than monoculture defaults.

The inventory must:

1. **Exclude** typefaces that have become AI-slop markers through overuse on generic SaaS / template ecosystems 2015-2024 (full exclusion list below — do not propose these or their close cousins).
2. **Include** typefaces that elite designers, award-winning agencies, and reputable foundries are demonstrably using in 2025-2026 production work — verified via primary sources, not derivative listicles.
3. **Cover** a balanced spread of registers (editorial, technical/SaaS, industrial/bold, warm/artisanal, brutalist/structural, expressive/display, neo-grotesque, humanist sans, modern serif, slab, monospace).
4. **Separate** Google Fonts (free, web-hostable via CDN) from boutique foundries (commercial license required, self-host).

# Context — what we are building

We operate a brand identity generator that, given a marketing brief, selects from a pool of ~50 display typefaces and ~50 body typefaces (per audacity tier 1/2/3). The pool is currently dominated by Google Fonts heritage popular fonts (Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Montserrat, DM Sans, Poppins, Playfair Display, Cormorant — all now AI-slop markers) and is missing the actual premium 2025-2026 references (Geist absent in display, Cabinet Grotesk absent everywhere, Switzer absent, etc.).

We need to rebuild the pool. Your output is the source material.

# Hard exclusion list — do not propose these, do not propose their close cousins

These typefaces are **banned absolutely** from the inventory because they have become invisible defaults / AI-slop markers (documented by Impeccable typography reference, Taste Skill, GStack design review, Vercel Web Interface Guidelines, Awwwards Perplexity report 2026):

**Invisible SaaS defaults (banned):** Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Montserrat, Poppins, Helvetica, Arial, Source Sans 3, Public Sans, Nunito Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans (borderline — discuss if 2025-2026 usage justifies inclusion), IBM Plex Sans, DM Sans

**Generic serifs (banned):** Times New Roman, Georgia, Garamond, Palatino, Book Antiqua, Bookman

**"Tasteful reflex" trapped fonts (banned — these are the LLM second-favorite trap):** Fraunces, Cormorant, Cormorant Garamond, Cormorant Infant, Playfair Display, DM Serif Display, DM Serif Text, EB Garamond (borderline — discuss)

**Cliché pairings (do not propose as pairings):** Playfair + Lato, Cormorant + Montserrat, Poppins + Open Sans, Inter + Inter Mono mono-stack, Georgia + Verdana

**Anti-cousin clause:** for each banned family, also avoid its visual cousins — fonts of the same era, same proportions, same geometric/humanist construction that would be the LLM's natural fallback. Example: banned Inter → do not propose Roboto Flex / Public Sans / Hanken Grotesk as close substitutes; propose typefaces with genuine distinctive character instead.

# Quality bar — what we want vs what we don't

**We want:**
- Typefaces with **demonstrable usage** by elite teams (Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Notion, design agencies winning Awwwards 2025-2026, premium editorial publications redesigning in 2024-2026)
- Typefaces with **distinctive character** — a brand designer can articulate *why this typeface and not another* in one sentence
- Typefaces with **sufficient weights / variable axes** to build hierarchy without pairing
- A **balanced spread** across registers — not 25 neo-grotesques and 5 serifs, but coverage of every common brand register

**We don't want:**
- Generic "popular Google Fonts 2024" listicle output (we have that already, that's the slop)
- Typefaces from foundries that are not actively used in production
- Typefaces selected on the basis of "well-known" / "everyone uses it" (that is precisely the slop signal)
- Fillers to reach the 30-40 count — rather output 25 excellent ones than 40 with 15 mediocre

# Adversarial check before submitting

Before producing the final answer, verify each row against this checklist:

1. Is this typeface in the hard exclusion list above? If yes, remove.
2. Is this typeface a close visual cousin of an excluded family (same era, same construction, same proportions)? If yes, remove unless the distinctiveness is documented.
3. Have I cited a specific 2024-2026 elite usage with a verifiable URL or named project? If no, demote or remove.
4. Does the sensorial register column actually distinguish this typeface, or am I using a vague label? If vague, reformulate.
5. Have I balanced the spread across registers, or am I over-representing tech/neo-grotesque?
